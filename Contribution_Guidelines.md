# Contributing Guidelines

Thank you for your interest in contributing to our project. To ensure the project remains legally sound, open, and free of intellectual property conflicts, all contributors must strictly adhere to the policies outlined below.

## Legal and Intellectual Property Policy

We maintain a zero-tolerance policy for copyright infringement and proprietary code contamination. Every line of code submitted must be legally unencumbered and fit for open-source distribution.

* **No Proprietary Code:** Do not submit proprietary code, trade secrets, or restricted binaries. All contributions must be original or explicitly compatible with the project's open-source licensing framework.
* **Strict Prohibition of Copyright Infringement:** Never copy, paste, or adapt code, header files, documentation, or firmware blobs from proprietary SDKs, vendor-closed drivers, or copyrighted sources. 
* **Compliant Reverse Engineering:** If you are contributing hardware support or drivers developed through reverse engineering, it must be performed strictly via lawful methods (such as clean-room design, interoperability research, or black-box protocol analysis). You must ensure your implementation contains no unauthorized proprietary source code or stolen binary fragments.

## Developer Certificate of Origin (DCO)

By contributing to this repository, you certify that:
1. The contribution was created in whole or in part by you, and you have the right to submit it under the project's license.
2. The contribution is based upon work that complies with our legal standards, including lawful reverse-engineering practices where applicable.
3. You understand that this project is public and that a permanent record of your contribution will be maintained.

## Contribution Workflow

1. **Fork and Clone:** Create your own fork of the repository and clone it locally.
2. **Create a Branch:** Develop your feature, fix, or driver in a dedicated branch (`git checkout -b feature/your-feature-name`).
3. **Verify Compliance:** Audit your code to ensure no proprietary artifacts, copyrighted snippets, or questionable dependencies have been introduced.
4. **Submit a Pull Request:** Open a pull request against the main branch with a detailed explanation of your changes and the methodology used for any hardware interfacing.
